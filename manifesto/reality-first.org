* Preamble

This is the manifesto of a working practice. It describes how to organize
any work that /accumulates against a reality and must be answered for/ —
a research program, a long engineering effort, a commissioned delivery,
a professional life — around two things: the *digital traces* the work
leaves behind, and the *automation* (today, language models) that can
absorb almost every cost of keeping those traces honest.

The practice in one paragraph:

#+begin_quote
Organize the work as a self-correcting loop. Action makes contact with
reality and leaves traces. Traces are the most authoritative account of
what happened; every representation built on them — map, roadmap, plan,
summary — is re-derived from the traces and disposable, and where
representation and traces disagree, the traces win. A machine does all
the friction around the loop: recording, distilling, surfacing,
narrating, scaffolding — because that friction is what historically
kills the practice. Exactly one node is never delegated: the situated
human judgment that turns surfaced reality into a committed choice and
signs for it. The representation's prescriptiveness is a dial, set by
how bounded the work is.
#+end_quote

Everything that follows introduces the concepts in that paragraph,
grounds them, names where they are fragile, and states what would prove
them wrong. The practice was not designed top-down; it was extracted
from several working systems — a personal memory log kept across years,
a data-science methodology, a compiler project, a bounded delivery
against a brief agreed in writing with its recipient — that converged
on the same design decisions independently. The manifesto distils
that convergence, with its corrections applied rather than hidden.

* The problem

Three failures motivate the practice. Each is old; the third is new.

** The articulation tax

Faithful records of work die from friction, not from lack of conviction.
The lab notebook decays into "we tried some stuff, it didn't work." The
engineering log goes stale the week a deadline lands. The journal stops
the day it becomes a chore. The cost of /articulating/ what happened —
writing it down, keeping it organized, keeping it findable — competes
with the work itself, and the work wins. The valuable practice is not
done badly; it stops being done at all.

** Map drift

Every standing representation of work — the project plan, the backlog,
the architecture document, the mental model — begins as a faithful
account and then quietly stops being one. Reality moves; the map is
groomed instead of re-derived; eventually the map is defended /against/
reality. A plan that has drifted is worse than no plan, because it
still carries authority. Whole traditions of failure live here: the
master plan that flattens what it cannot see, the metric that replaces
the goal it was meant to measure, the backlog item nobody can trace to
an actual requirement.

** The new temptation: wholesale delegation

Language models changed the economics of both failures at once. They
can absorb the articulation tax almost entirely — and they can also,
for the first time, plausibly absorb the /judgment/. The same machine
that will happily maintain your log will happily write your
reflections, propose your hypotheses, and make your decisions, fluently
enough that the difference is invisible on the page. So the old
question — "how do we keep records honest?" — acquires a new, sharper
companion: /what, exactly, must not be delegated, and why?/

The reality-first practice is one answer to both questions together.

* The two principles

The practice rests on two principles. They are independent — neither
can be derived from the other — and the practice is their
/conjunction/. Getting their relationship right matters, because each
alone collapses into something that already exists and does not need a
manifesto.

** Principle I — the architecture: representations are answerable to traces

Picture someone charting an unknown coast from a small boat.

The explorer's golden rule: /the chart is always redrawn from the
coastline — never the coastline "corrected" to match the chart./ If the
map says "bay here" and you sail up to solid cliff, you do not argue
with the cliff; you cross out the bay and draw the cliff. The sea is
the truth; the chart is disposable notes about it. A captain who trusts
his beautiful old chart over the rocks in front of him drowns.

Stated precisely, the principle is an *authority ordering among
representations*. Pure "reality" is never directly in hand — what is in
hand are /traces/: the commit, the test result, the signed contract,
the measurement. Traces are themselves representations — selected,
encoded residues, not the territory. But they are the /least-derived/
account of reality available, and the principle is that authority flows
strictly down the derivation chain:

#+begin_quote
reality → traces → derived representations (maps, plans, summaries)

Each layer is answerable to the one above it, and is re-derived from
it, never the reverse. Where layers disagree, the less-derived layer
wins.
#+end_quote

This is how the sciences already handle theory-laden data — data
outranks theory even though data is never raw — and it is honest in a
way the slogan "reality wins" is not. The trace is not the territory
either; it is simply the closest thing to it that can sit in a file.

Three commitments follow directly:

- *Append-only traces.* History is never rewritten. A correction is a
  new trace that supersedes an old one visibly, not an edit that makes
  the past agree with the present.
- *Disposable representations.* Any map must be deletable and
  rebuildable from the traces. If it cannot be regenerated, it has
  become an authority of its own.
- *Re-derivation as a habit.* The map is not checked against reality
  when convenient; re-contact is a discipline with a cadence, because
  drift compounds in the gaps.

** Principle II — the allocation policy: one node is non-delegable

The second principle governs the division of labor between the human
and the machine, and it draws the line in an unexpected place: not at
difficulty, but at *authorship-dependence*.

A simple test finds the line. Imagine the act performed by someone — or
something — else, with a /provably identical/ result, and ask whether
you would take that deal.

- *The dishwasher.* Would you let a machine wash your dishes if they
  came out exactly as clean? Obviously yes. The clean dishes were the
  only thing you wanted; who produced them is irrelevant. The value is
  in the output → *delegate*, gladly, even though it is the bulk of the
  chore.
- *The marathon.* Would you pay someone to run your marathon for you
  and pin the medal on /you/ — same finish time, your name in the
  results? Obviously not. The medal was never the point; running it
  was. An identical outcome leaves nothing of value behind. The value
  is in the doing → *keep* it.

Both are tedious, sweaty, hours of effort — so the line between them is
/not difficulty/. In the dishes, the effort is the *price* you pay for
the result; in the marathon, the effort *is* the result. The same test
settles the cases this practice cares about: would you hire a
ghostwriter for your wedding vows, even if they moved the room to tears
just as much? No — vows are worthless unless they are yours. That "no"
is the same "no" as letting a model author your reflection, your
scientific judgment, or your sign-off.

The distinction is ancient. Aristotle separated /poiesis/ — production,
whose end is the product — from /praxis/ — action, whose end is in the
doing itself. The allocation policy in one line: *delegate the poiesis,
keep the praxis.* A line of code is authorship-independent — any
competent agent yields an equivalent line — so it is fully delegable,
even when it is the bulk of the visible, skilled labour. The design
choice and the sign-off are authorship-dependent, so they stay. The
machine may write every line; the human decides what to build and signs
that it was built right.

What stays human, concretely, is one node: *the situated judgment that
metabolizes surfaced reality into a committed choice, and signs for it.*
Signing is the act that constitutes you as the one answerable — and
because you sign, you must inspect what you sign with distrust, deep
inspection, and a critical spirit, as you would review external work
that goes out under your name.

** Neither alone: why the practice is the conjunction

Each principle, taken alone, names something that already exists.

- *The loop without the judgment node* is just cybernetics. A
  thermostat runs Principle I perfectly — re-contact reality, correct
  the representation — with no witness, no signature, no judgment. Most
  control systems do. Nothing new there.
- *The judgment without the loop* is just record-keeping. A signed
  affidavit is an answerable, authored account with no loop at all.
  Nothing new there either.

What is distinctive is the conjunction: a self-correcting loop between
reality and a disposable representation, /in which the one node that is
not automated is the situated human judgment/ — while everything else
is offloaded to a machine. And the two principles need each other in
both directions:

- *Principle II selects the domain where Principle I matters.* The loop
  applies everywhere; the /practice/ applies exactly where the value of
  the output depends on someone being answerable for it over time.
  Where nothing must be answered for, run the thermostat — full
  automation is correct, and this manifesto does not apply (see Scope
  limits).
- *Principle I is what makes Principle II's node trustworthy.* The
  judgment literature is blunt about this: intuitive expert judgment is
  reliable only in environments that provide valid cues and fast,
  honest feedback (Kahneman & Klein, 2009). An unlooped judge drifts
  exactly like an unlooped map. The loop — re-contact, re-derive,
  compare — is what /calibrates/ the human comparator. The judge needs
  the loop as much as the loop needs the judge.

One more asymmetry, worth stating because it dates this document
honestly: *Principle I is timeless; Principle II is era-dependent.* The
direction of authority between map and territory does not move as
machines improve. The /location of the fence/ does — work that was kept
human in one capability regime becomes delegable in the next (writing
code already crossed; proposing hypotheses may cross). Principle II is
therefore not a fixed list of protected tasks but a *policy*: at any
moment, delegate everything authorship-independent, however hard, and
keep the choice and the signature. The policy is stable; its extension
moves with the machines.

* The vocabulary

The practice needs few terms, but it needs them exactly. Eight are
load-bearing; everything else can be said in ordinary words.

- *World-trace* :: the residue of /reality/: the commit, the signed
  contract, the test result, the meeting's actual outcome. The
  authoritative layer. World-traces have veto power over every derived
  representation, and a /changed/ world-trace is the one thing allowed
  to interrupt you and pull the framing upstream.
- *Signal* :: an incoming stimulus that is not yet a trace of anything:
  an email arrives, an idea is floated, a thought occurs. A signal
  changes nothing until you form intent and act on it. Treating every
  signal as authoritative floods the judgment with noise.
- *Work-trace* :: the residue of /your activity/: the conversation you
  had, the decision you recorded, the dead end you explored. Provenance
  and lineage — narrative, not setpoint.
- *Comparator* :: the human node of the loop: the one who holds the
  derived view against the traces, judges, chooses, and signs. The word
  is borrowed from control theory, with one correction noted below.
- *Setpoint* :: what the loop regulates toward. For the account itself:
  "the representation faithfully matches the traces." For bounded work:
  the deadline and the contract — which are themselves world-traces.
- *Phantom* :: an item in a derived representation whose provenance
  reaches no world-trace. A requirement nobody actually imposed, a
  belief with no residue behind it. Phantoms are the characteristic
  failure of standing maps, and the provenance discipline below exists
  to make them visible: they are the nodes with no root.
- *The spine* :: the one append-only narrative log of the practitioner
  across all work — identity, durable learnings, accumulated judgment.
  Singular, map-less, and never rewritten.
- *Derived view* (or /roadmap/) :: any disposable representation
  re-derived from traces: a project roadmap, a status summary, an
  index. Plural, local, and deletable by construction.

* The loop

** The shape

#+begin_src
   action  ─────────────▶  traces  (contact with reality)
      ▲                       │
      │                       ▼
      │       distil into checkpoint documents                   (encode)
      │                       │
      │                       ▼
      │          recall context by situation                     (retrieve)
      │                       │
      │                       ▼
      └─────────  committed, signed choice  ◀──  the comparator  (judge)
#+end_src

Action leaves traces. Traces are /encoded/: distilled into checkpoint
documents — not chronological records but crystallisations, engineered
for a dual audience, serving at once as the human's resumption point
and as portable context that primes a model to reason over the whole
picture. Checkpoints are /retrieved/ by situation, not loaded
wholesale: the project you are in, the threads that are fresh, the
horizon you are on cue what comes back into view. (This design is
modeled on the encoding-specificity findings of the memory literature —
retrieval works best when the cues at recall match the cues at encoding
— as a guiding analogy, not a claimed implementation.) Retrieval
informs the /judgment/ — the non-delegable node — which issues new
action, which leaves new traces.

The machine operates on every arc except the judgment: it distils, it
recalls, it narrates. It does not judge. And the loop re-contacts
reality on every pass — which is why the account cannot quietly drift
into elaborating its own prior plan.

** The systems reading: a balancing loop governing a reinforcing one

In systems-dynamics vocabulary (Meadows), the practice is two coupled
loops, and the whole design is in the coupling.

The /governing/ loop is *balancing* — textbook negative feedback. Stock:
the fidelity of the representation to the traces. Setpoint: "the
account matches reality." Error signal: the gap between map and traces.
Corrective flow: traces win, the map is pulled back.

The loop /worth running/ is *reinforcing*. Define *reach* as the span
of reality a single practitioner can faithfully account for — across
time and across parallel threads. Each honest cycle enlarges the
account, which improves retrieval, which sharpens judgment, which
produces more faithful action, which enlarges the account again. Reach
compounds. That compounding is the point of the practice — it is what
"extending human reach" means, mechanically.

#+begin_src
      ┌─────────── R: reach compounds (the point) ────────────┐
      ▼                                                       │
   action ─▶ traces ─▶ [re-derive] ─▶ judgment ─▶ commit ─▶ action
                          ▲   │
                          │   ▼
                    B: the comparator
              (map vs traces → traces win)
             setpoint = fidelity to reality
#+end_src

The load-bearing insight is pure systems theory: *a reinforcing loop
left ungoverned runs away.* Without the balancing loop, reach does not
compound fidelity — it compounds /drift/: the account keeps growing,
but grows away from reality, larger and less true. The practice is a
designed inverse of the classic "limits to growth" trap: instead of
letting the reinforcing engine slam into an external limit, you install
the balancing loop internally, on purpose, as a governor. *R is why you
bother; B is what keeps R honest.*

Two of the practice's habits are, in these terms, interventions at the
highest-leverage points:

- *Re-derive from traces every session* is /shrinking the perception
  delay/. Delays in balancing loops cause overshoot and oscillation; a
  map that is not resampled for weeks accumulates error silently.
- *Traces win over the map* is the anti-Goodhart rule: regulate against
  the least-derived account available, never against the
  representation you are fond of. Balancing loops fail precisely when
  they control against the believed state instead of the actual one.

** A phantom, caught: the loop working once

One real incident shows the whole mechanism. A bounded delivery
against an agreed brief carried a deliverable — a separate dossier of
source documents — that had entered as a /self-imposed idea/ in a
working session and been encoded into the roadmap /as if the brief
required it/. It survived there for weeks, untethered. The fix was
exactly the loop: a session-opening re-contact with the actual
world-trace (the brief, agreed in writing with its recipient before
the work began) revealed the map had grown a requirement reality
never imposed. The item was struck through — not deleted — with the date and
the reason, so the correction is itself a trace.

Every element of the practice appears in that story: the phantom (an
item whose provenance reached no world-trace), the perception delay
(weeks without resampling), the corrective habit (verify the real state
at session opening), and the append-only repair (strikethrough, never
erasure). Drift is not hypothetical; it is the standing failure mode of
every prescriptive document, and the only defence is the habit of
re-deriving from traces.

** What the law of requisite variety does and does not prove

It is tempting to ground the human node in Ashby's law of requisite
variety — a regulator can control a system only if it commands at least
as much variety as the disturbances it must absorb — and conclude that
the comparator /must/ be human, as if by theorem. The law proves no
such thing. It is completely neutral about what kind of system the
regulator is; if a machine attains requisite variety for a domain,
Ashby /licenses/ automating that domain's comparator.

What the law actually supplies is the *criterion the fence answers to*:
the comparator must command variety matching the reality it judges —
including everything tacit, situated, and contextual that never made it
into the traces. The claim that, for answerable work, only the situated
human currently meets that criterion is an /empirical, dated/ claim —
supported today by what we know of tacit knowledge (Polanyi) and expert
judgment (Dreyfus), and honestly subject to revision. This is exactly
why Principle II is a policy rather than a theorem, why the fence is
held as a norm rather than a mechanism, and why its location moves
between capability regimes while the architecture of Principle I does
not.

* The division of labor

** The two machine seats

The machine sits in two distinct seats, on either side of the human
node.

- *The interlocutor* operates on the /forming/ of the decision:
  divergent first (widen the view), then convergent (narrow it), then
  adversarial (attack the assumptions). Properly understood it is a
  *stimulator*: the diverging and converging happen in the human's
  head; the model is the provocation that widens the aperture and the
  foil that surfaces hidden assumptions — a stimulus to cognition,
  never its replacement.
- *The chronicler* operates on the /recording/ of the work: making
  dense traces — code, choices, dead ends — legible; translating
  technical work into a followable story; re-rendering the derived
  views. The model is the chronicler, not the author.

The human's non-delegable acts sit precisely /between/ the two seats:
the model helps you form intent and helps you record what happened; it
is fenced out of the instant where you actually choose and the instant
where you actually witness.

The seats also give the practice a temporal shape. At /inception/, the
interlocutor earns its keep: one or more sessions that diverge across
the problem space and converge on a crystallised problem statement and
a roadmap. In /execution/, the chronicler and the loop take over:
recording, recalling, re-projecting, with the roadmap kept honest by
re-derivation. The choice brackets the front; the sign-off brackets the
end.

** The registered prior

The interlocutor seat carries a danger the early versions of this
practice underestimated: *the menu is the decision.* Decades of
judgment research (anchoring; choice architecture) show that whoever
generates the option set substantially steers the choice, even when the
chooser formally decides. A model that frames your options has done
much of your deciding, invisibly, while leaving the signature to you.
The fence around the final pick does not protect the agenda.

The countermeasure is a discipline: *write your own position down
before invoking the model.* State what you currently believe, what you
expect, what you would do absent advice — then open the dialogue. The
stimulator then widens an existing aperture instead of framing an empty
one, and you can see afterwards exactly where it moved you. Ask for the
case /against/ your registered position before asking for options. A
prior you never wrote down is a prior the model writes for you.

** Delegate by authorship, not difficulty

Everything authorship-independent is handed to the machine eagerly —
and "everything" is meant literally, including the bulk of the visible,
skilled labour. Surfacing, orienting, indexing, formatting,
summarizing-for-orientation, scaffolding, drafting, and in software the
writing of nearly every line of code: all of it is poiesis, valued by
its output, delegable in full. +Partial delegation of the delegable
layer is not caution; it is a babysitting tax that frees no one.+
/(Struck 2026-08-30: true of delegation aimed at the output; not of
delegable work retained to calibrate the judge — see fragility five.)/

What is kept is praxis: the choice of what to build, the judgment
against reality, the reflection, the signature. Kept not because the
machine cannot produce convincing versions of them — it increasingly
can — but because a convincing version produced by a non-witness is
worth nothing. The account derives its value from who answers for it.

** The oracle, refused a seat

The model invites a third use, distinct from both seats: the *oracle*.
The two seats ask it to work on what you already command — you can
judge the output, so delegation is safe by construction. The oracle
use asks it what you do /not/ know, and there the danger is routinely
misdiagnosed. It is not that asking is wrong, nor even that the model
may be wrong. It is that an oracle's answer is a derived
representation whose provenance reaches no world-trace — and the
moment you act on it unchecked, it becomes authoritative: a phantom at
birth. Oracle use is the map-first inversion re-entering through the
window — a non-answerable representation outranking reality — inside
the very practice built to prevent it.

The operative question is therefore never "is the model reliable
here?" — that question has no honest answer at the moment you need
one. The question is: /does this answer have a path to reality before
I sign on it?/ Two zones follow:

- *Convertible.* You cannot judge the answer directly, but you can
  make it touch reality before signing: run the code, check that the
  cited source exists and says what is claimed, do the experiment. The
  loop closes through traces, and the answer enters the system the
  only way anything may — as a trace of contact, not as an utterance
  taken on authority.
- *Unclosable.* No affordable contact with reality stands between the
  answer and the action. The loop cannot close, and the model's
  representation quietly takes the setpoint's place.

The rule in one line: *a model's answer is a signal, never a trace.*
It may provoke intent like any other signal; it earns authority only
through contact with reality. Where no such contact is affordable, the
honest moves are to record the ground as what it is — "acting on the
model's unverified claim, because X" — or not to act at all.

* The artifact spectrum

The practice does not prescribe one artifact. It prescribes a *dial*,
set by two axes of the work.

** Axis one: bounded ↔ open-ended

How delivery-bounded is the work?

- *Bounded delivery* — a defined end-state, a deadline, phases,
  something to sign. Here the right artifact is the *live emergent
  document* (below): a roadmap that is descriptive and prescriptive at
  once, re-derived from the traces, driving toward an end.
- *Open-ended accumulation* — no end-state, no deliverable; identity
  and judgment forming across years. Here a standing prescriptive map
  is exactly the liability to refuse: the right artifact is the pure
  append-only *spine*, with views derived only at review time and then
  discarded.
- *Between* — work that is open-ended overall but bounded per chapter
  (a research program: questions arise indefinitely, but each
  hypothesis has a falsifiable end) maintains a map scoped to the
  bounded unit and nothing wider.

The artifact's /prescriptiveness/ slides along this axis; the loop and
the judgment node are invariant across the whole range. A methodology
is not better or worse in the abstract — it shines on the problem it
was shaped to and dims when lifted out of it. Choose the artifact by
the shape of the work, not by allegiance to a method.

** Axis two: well-modeled ↔ novel

There is an honest counter-tradition this practice must answer rather
than ignore: in some regimes, /plan-primacy wins/. Aviation checklists,
building codes, surgical protocols — standing prescriptive maps that
systematically outperform situated judgment in their domains, with
strong evidence behind them. Reality-first is not a universal law.

The resolution is a second axis: *how well-modeled is the domain?*
Where the domain model is mature, failure modes are enumerated, and
novelty is low, the map has earned authority — follow the checklist,
and let deviation from it be the thing that requires judgment and a
signature. Where the domain model is poor, the work is novel, or the
territory is still being charted, the map cannot have earned that
authority, and reality-first is the only honest stance. Most knowledge
work — research, design, writing, early engineering — lives at the
novel end. But the practice should say plainly: the explorer redraws
the chart /because the coast is unknown/. Once a coast is charted to
death, you follow the chart, and that too is fidelity to reality.

** The live emergent document

For bounded work, the central artifact is a single living roadmap with
three properties:

- *Descriptive and prescriptive at once.* It mirrors the current state
  of the project, re-rendered from the traces each time it is touched —
  and precisely /because/ it is current, it can legitimately say what
  to do next. It is prescriptive only for as long as it stays honestly
  descriptive; the moment it decrees rather than reflects, drift has
  crept in.
- *Emergent.* It is not authored top-down as a plan; it condenses out
  of the accumulating traces and shifts as they shift.
- *Disposable.* It is a regenerable view: delete it and rebuild it from
  the traces. That deletability is the architectural guarantee that it
  never becomes the thing you are loyal to.

Under delivery pressure the clean separation of "log upstream, map
downstream" collapses into a single file carrying three strata at once,
and this is correct, not a compromise:

- an *append-only decision ledger* — each shipped item bound to its
  evidence (a commit hash, a date, a rationale), each scope decision
  frozen with its date;
- *corrections that preserve their own history* — a superseded plan is
  struck through with the date and the reason, never deleted, so the
  revision is itself testimony;
- a *live forward projection* — the phase breakdown, the verified
  current state, the "remains to be done."

** Roadmaps are plural and local; the spine is singular and map-less

The granularity rule: *one balancing loop → one roadmap.* The unit is
not "project" or "team" but /the unit with a single setpoint and a
single signer/. Most projects are one deadline-shaped setpoint and get
one roadmap. A sub-part earns its own roadmap only when it is itself a
bounded sub-delivery with its own falsifiable end and its own owner; a
mere phase of the same delivery is a section, not a new map. Where the
horizon splits is where a new roadmap buds.

The placement rule: *roadmaps live next to the traces they are derived
from* — in the project's own repository or workspace — never
centralised in an orchestrator. Re-derivation must be local and cheap;
"traces win" must be a low-latency check; and no central store of maps
should ever compete with the spine for authority. The spine — the one
open-ended log of the practitioner across all work — holds only what no
single workspace can hold: identity, durable learnings distilled up
from closed projects, and a /derived/ index of what is fresh, active,
and dormant. The spine *references, never duplicates*: it finds the
roadmaps; it does not keep them. When a project closes, its roadmap is
disposable — distil the durable learnings up into the spine and let the
map dissolve. The reach stays; the scaffolding goes.

** Evidence: one durable home for every trace

Every world-trace a decision rests on needs /one authoritative,
re-contactable home/, so the derived views can be checked against it.
Three tiers, by the durability of the referent:

1. *Durable and addressable → reference it.* A commit, a file, the
   signed contract, a stable ticket. Point at it; a copy would start
   drifting from the original.
2. *Ephemeral or external → capture it.* Email threads, screenshots,
   saved PDFs, pasted excerpts — into a per-project =traces/= (or
   =evidence/=) folder, each item carrying when it was captured and
   which decision it grounds. Here the copy /is/ the faithful move: it
   does not drift from a living referent, it prevents the referent from
   vanishing.
3. *No footprint at all → externalise at the intent statement.* A
   hallway conversation, a verbal commitment, a realisation: write it
   down as you commit — "doing X because Y told me Z, <date>." That
   line becomes the world-trace.

This is deliberate, scoped capture by the comparator of the specific
evidence a decision rests on — the opposite of ambient ingestion.
Pulled deliberately and scoped to a decision: yes. Pushed automatically
as an inbox: refused. And the capture discipline has a scope rule:
capture what is /decision-relevant/, not everything — the cure for
forgetting is better cue-matching, not more storage.

One case deserves naming rather than faking: some grounds are *tacit*.
You know more than you can tell, and some judgments rest on experience
that has no externalisable footprint. The honest move is to record the
tacit ground /as such/ — "doing X on my judgment, from experience with
similar cases" — rather than fabricating a documentary basis. That
node is un-auditable by design, because it /is/ the witnessing act.
Recording it honestly is better provenance than pretending.

** Provenance: the audit graph

Every artifact — a roadmap line, a todo, an idea, a decision — carries
two links: a *down-link* to its origin (which world-trace or signal it
came from) and an *up-link* to what it became (the intent or action it
fed). The graph this closes is the drift-detector: an item whose
down-link never reaches a world-trace is a phantom, flagged for
pruning; a backlog item whose originating signal has gone stale is a
candidate for review-time retirement.

And one asymmetry, which is Principle I in miniature: *world-traces may
trigger re-entry; the backlog may not.* A failing test, a drifted
metric, a re-read contract clause — a changed world-trace is allowed to
interrupt and pull you upstream, because it is the setpoint. Todos and
ideas merely /surface/ when you orient, and wait to be chosen. A
backlog that fires on its own is a map decreeing action — the exact
inversion the practice exists to prevent. Intent is *formed at the
confluence* of verified reality, surfaced signals, and consulted
candidates; it is never /derived from/ any artifact. The moment a todo
"generates" the next action, the comparator's job has been quietly
handed to the map.

* The daily practice

A session is a cognitive block, not a day. Inside it you walk the loop
once; the two human bookends are the non-delegable node.

1. *Orient — re-contact reality first.* Before trusting any document,
   resample the world: the log, the files, the test counts, what the
   contract actually says. Diff that against the roadmap; where they
   disagree, the traces win. This is the balancing correction firing at
   session start — minimizing the perception delay, the single
   highest-value habit in the practice.
2. *Surface — by situation, not everything.* Pull into view only what
   this moment cues: this project, fresh threads, the relevant horizon.
3. *Form intent — the comparator, with the stimulator.* Write your
   registered prior. Then let the model widen, narrow, and attack it.
   Then /you/ commit to a choice, sign it, and record its provenance —
   which world-traces it regulates against, which signals and
   candidates it consulted, which grounds are tacit.
4. *Act — the only writer.* Do the work; hand the machine every
   authorship-independent piece. Action leaves traces. Nothing enters
   the system's state except through action.
5. *Chronicle — the machine distils.* It compresses the session's dense
   traces into legible narrative and re-renders the derived views. You
   sign the chronicle.
6. *Reflect — the comparator again.* Intent versus outcome, plus
   residue: what did this session leave behind that matters beyond it?
   Append to the spine; promote durable learnings out of the disposable
   map.

Rhythm: *orient → surface → intent → act → chronicle → reflect.* The
friction in the middle belongs to the machine; the two ends — choosing
and witnessing — are yours.

Two refinements:

- *The =contradict= move.* A standing use of the model whose only job
  is to surface /what contradicts the current framing/ — never to
  maintain or polish it. This is the deliberate countermeasure to the
  model's own fluency (fragility three, below): an agent pointed at the
  cracks, because the default direction of a helpful model is to paper
  them over. The verdict on a surfaced contradiction stays with the
  comparator — it is structurally safer to delegate /finding/ a
  contradiction than to delegate /judging it resolved/.
- *Cadenced re-contact is legitimate for bounded work.* The purist "the
  human triggers everything" is right for the open-ended spine. But a
  deadline is itself a world-trace, and a scheduled re-orient is not
  ambient ingestion — it is shrinking the perception delay. The cadence
  resamples reality; the comparator still forms every intent. What
  stays forbidden is any automation that /decides/ for you.

* The five fragilities

The practice is load-bearing but brittle in five places. Naming them is
part of the practice; each has a countermeasure, and none has a
guarantee.

1. *The fence is a norm, not a mechanism.* Nothing technically stops
   you from rubber-stamping a machine-drafted reflection or accepting
   "the data suggests X" as your judgment. The moment you do, the
   artifact silently stops being yours while looking identical. There
   is no runtime check — only discipline, and the adversarial
   inspection that signing demands. As models grow more persuasive, the
   gradient across the fence steepens. The failure is invisible and
   self-inflicted.
2. *Some fences are thin.* Where the machine is plausibly capable of
   the protected act — proposing the hypothesis, ranking the options —
   the line between "orienting you" and "making the call" narrows. The
   discipline is to have the model /narrate options without ranking
   them/ where the choice is the protected act, and to notice when
   orientation has quietly become recommendation.
3. *Fluency papers over the cracks.* The practice depends on reality
   being allowed to overturn the framing — and a model that is too good
   at making the existing plan feel coherent will smooth exactly the
   contradictions that should send you back upstream. The model's
   fluency is itself a risk to the practice's empiricism. Countermeasure:
   the =contradict= move, run as a habit, plus the session-opening
   re-derivation from traces — not cleverer maintenance.
4. *The menu is the decision.* Whoever generates the option set steers
   the choice, even when the human formally decides. The fence protects
   the final pick but not the agenda — and agenda-setting is where much
   of the steering power lives. Countermeasure: the registered prior,
   written before the model speaks, and the standing request for the
   case /against/ it.
5. *The comparator drifts, and nothing in the loop catches it.* The
   loop as described recalibrates only the map: re-derive, diff,
   correct. No arc recalibrates the judge — yet the judge's variety
   comes from having done the work (Polanyi again: tacit knowledge is
   acquired in the doing, not in the watching), and total delegation
   of the doing silently switches off the feedback loop by which
   judgment is maintained. This is fragility one with a structural
   cause — eroded competence — in place of a moral one, and it is
   undetectable by construction: rubber-stamped output looks identical
   to inspected output, and the correction rate is ambiguous at both
   ends. A falling rate is ambiguous between "the machine improved"
   and "I stopped seeing." A high rate on a task recorded as delegated
   is ambiguous between "the machine is failing" and "I am doing the
   work through it and crediting it" — the Clever Hans effect, as
   Kambhampati (2024) observes of human-in-the-loop prompting: the
   horse that appeared to count while reading its handler, and the
   handler steering "even if they didn't set out to do so
   deliberately." The effect is defined by being unnoticed, so a
   tracked rate catches only its visible half; a back-prompt, a
   reworded ask, an "are you sure about step three?" is a correction
   round and is chronicled as one. The first ambiguity misattributes
   the machine's work to the judge; the second misattributes the
   judge's work to the machine, and it corrupts the evidence Principle
   II moves on — the fence is moved outward on the experience that the
   machine now does the work. The test is not when the judge
   contributed but what the contribution carried: the line runs
   between a prompt that specifies the problem and a prompt that
   injects its solution, whenever either is issued. What has crossed
   is only what the machine added beyond what the prompts carried; a
   spec so complete that the machine only formats it delegates the
   formatting and nothing more. An act has crossed only when the
   machine's output passes a sound check the judge did not author the
   answer to, and the chronicle records what the prompts carried, not
   the word "delegated." (Kambhampati's closing point, that the
   arrangement becomes questionable when the human cannot verify the
   answer, is the unclosable zone of the oracle rule.) The better
   the delegation, the less informative the experience of supervising
   it (Bainbridge's irony of automation). The serious counterargument
   — the critic does not paint, the editor does not write novels —
   cuts the other way on inspection: those evaluators were formed by
   decades of feedback loops aimed at evaluation itself, with
   consequences for being wrong. Judgment is a stock maintained only
   by its own loop. Countermeasure: a /second balancing loop, pointed
   at the judge/ — deliberate re-contact with the delegated layer
   (periodically do by hand what you normally delegate, not for the
   output but to resample your own competence: the inward analogue of
   the session-opening re-derivation); seeded errors, the blind
   proficiency test (deliberately defective output placed among the
   genuine, to measure whether detection still fires); and a tracked
   correction rate whose decline is treated as a signal to
   disambiguate, never as a success, and whose height is treated as a
   signal that the task has not crossed, never as delegation. The
   practice began by naming two
   things that decay — the map and the account. There is a third: the
   comparator. Each needs its own corrective loop.

* Scope limits

The practice is strong but not universal. It holds where work
/accumulates/ against a reality and someone must /answer/ for it over
time. It weakens or inverts elsewhere, and pretending otherwise would
be the kind of map this practice refuses.

- *Throughput work with no accumulating reality.* Tasks valued purely
  by volume and speed — bulk translation, routine triage, content at
  scale — have no account to author and no framing for reality to
  overturn. Here substitution /is/ the goal, and this apparatus is dead
  weight.
- *Disposable generation.* A throwaway prototype, a first-draft
  brainstorm, a scaffold meant to be deleted: nothing will ever be
  signed, so there is no authorship to protect. Let the machine author
  it outright; insisting on the fence would be ceremony.
- *Speed-over-trust domains.* Where fast and approximately right beats
  slow and accountable, the cost structure that makes the practice
  worthwhile inverts.
- *Well-modeled, checklist-governed regimes.* Where the domain model
  has earned authority (axis two, above), follow the map and reserve
  judgment for deviations. Plan-primacy is not the enemy; /unearned/
  plan-primacy is.
- *Multi-signer work.* The practice as stated is a solo discipline:
  one loop, one setpoint, one signer. Nothing here is proven for two
  comparators sharing traces — whose signature binds, how registered
  priors compose, what a fence held by a committee is worth. The
  institutional record is not encouraging: where inspection of machine
  output has been mandated at scale, it has mostly produced the ritual
  rather than the inspection (Green, 2022). Scaling the fence is an
  open problem, stated here as a limit rather than smuggled in as a
  feature; the transfer test comes first.

The boundary in one line: /the practice applies exactly where the value
of the output depends on someone being answerable for it over time,/
/in territory not yet charted well enough for the chart to rule./

* Falsifiability

A manifesto that demands fidelity to reality owes reality the terms of
its own surrender. The practice is refuted if any of the following
holds at scale and over time:

- *Delegated judgment does not degrade the work.* A practice in which
  the machine authors the reflection, the scientific decision, or the
  sign-off — and the resulting body of work proves as trustworthy,
  deep, and durable as the witnessed version — falsifies the
  non-delegability claim. The honest test is over time: substitution
  often looks free until the account must be returned to, defended, or
  built upon.
- *Opacity beats legibility.* If opaque, database-backed systems
  reliably outperform plain-text trace architectures on both longevity
  and human-machine reasoning quality, the substrate commitment is
  wrong.
- *Hand-groomed maps win at scale.* If maintained prescriptive maps
  systematically outperform regenerated views once regeneration is
  cheap, the disposability commitment is mistaken rather than
  principled.
- *Inspection is ritual.* If practitioners who sign whatever the model
  emits show no loss in outcome quality, then "responsibility draws the
  line" describes a ceremony, not a mechanism.

To keep these testable rather than rhetorical, the practice proposes
its own measures:

- *Resumption time*: how long to regain full working context on a
  project untouched for three months — the loop claims this shrinks
  dramatically.
- *Phantom count*: requirements discovered to trace to no world-trace,
  per project, with and without the provenance discipline.
- *Signed versus rubber-stamped*: defect and reversal rates of
  decisions that went through the registered-prior-plus-inspection path
  versus decisions that did not.
- *Seeded-error detection*: the rate at which deliberately defective
  machine output, placed blind among the genuine, is caught at
  inspection — the one unambiguous measure of whether signing is
  inspection or ceremony.
- *Correction rate over time*: how often the comparator corrects the
  machine's output, tracked in the knowledge that a decline is
  ambiguous between model improvement and comparator drift — to be
  disambiguated (seeded errors are the disambiguator), never read as
  a success by itself; and that a high rate on a task recorded as
  delegated is ambiguous between machine failure and Clever Hans —
  the judge doing the work through the machine — to be disambiguated
  by the sound check (does the output pass without the judge
  correcting it, and without the asking having carried the answer?),
  never read as delegation.
- *The transfer test*: whether the practice survives adoption by a
  second practitioner without its originator's coaching.

And one limitation stated plainly, because the practice demands it: the
evidence base to date is a handful of projects by a single
practitioner. Convergence across one person's projects is excellent
/hypothesis generation/ and weak /confirmation/ — a stable personal
style would produce the same convergence. The manifesto is offered as a
falsifiable proposal, not a proven result. The transfer test is the
next experiment.

Since the first draft, external evidence has begun to arrive, and
honesty requires logging it on both sides. On the cost of delegated
cognition: a survey of 319 knowledge workers finds that the more a
worker trusts the model, the less critical-thinking effort they
report investing (Lee et al., CHI 2025); an EEG study of LLM-assisted
essay writing finds accumulating "cognitive debt," with assisted
writers unable to quote from their own essays and reporting impaired
ownership of them (Kosmyna et al., 2025) — the marathon medal,
measured. On the fence as ritual: the empirical record on mandated
human oversight of algorithms finds that people largely cannot
perform it, and that policy requiring it mostly produces the ceremony
(Green, 2022). None of this settles the falsifiers; all of it bears
on them, and in the direction the practice predicts — while also
documenting how often real practitioners fail to hold the fence.

* Lineage

The practice is a synthesis, not an invention; naming the ancestry
situates it and clarifies the one new thing. The inclusion test for
this list is strict: a source earns its place by /doing work/ —
generating a distinction, constraining a decision, or naming a failure
mode — not by resonating. (That test is the practice applied to its own
bibliography: an undiscriminated everything-connects reading list is
itself an ungoverned reinforcing loop.)

** The engine: representations answerable to reality

- *Alfred Korzybski* (/Science and Sanity/, 1933) — "the map is not the
  territory"; abstraction loses information at every level. The literal
  statement of Principle I, applied here recursively: the trace is not
  the territory either, only the least-derived map of it.
- *Karl Popper* (/Conjectures and Refutations/, 1963) — the loop as
  epistemology: knowledge grows by exposing representations to refuting
  contact with reality, never by protecting them. The session-opening
  re-derivation is conjecture-and-refutation as a habit; the phantom
  deliverable is a Lakatosian degenerating programme caught in the act.
  (David Deutsch's "good explanations are hard to vary" is the modern
  restatement.)
- *W. Ross Ashby* (/Design for a Brain/, 1952) — the law of requisite
  variety, used here /correctly/: it supplies the criterion the
  comparator must meet, not a theorem that the comparator must be
  human.
- *Gregory Bateson* (/Steps to an Ecology of Mind/, 1972) —
  "information is a difference that makes a difference" is what the
  comparator detects; circular causation and the map/territory relation
  are the loop's backbone.
- *Donella Meadows* (/Thinking in Systems/, 2008) — the working
  vocabulary: stocks, flows, setpoints, delays, leverage points; the
  insight that the practice's rules sit at the highest-leverage points
  (the goal, the paradigm, the information flows).
- *John Boyd* — the OODA loop (observe–orient–decide–act): the daily
  rhythm of this practice is a close cousin, independently derived in
  the military register, with "orient" as the pivotal and most
  misunderstood step. A practice-tradition confirmation that
  re-contacting reality faster than your representation decays is the
  whole game.
- *W. Edwards Deming and the Toyota tradition* — plan-do-check-act,
  /genchi genbutsu/ ("go and see for yourself at the actual place"),
  the gemba walk. The industrial orthopraxy of re-contacting reality,
  with decades of at-scale evidence — the strongest external validation
  that the loop works outside one practitioner's projects.
- *Event sourcing* (software architecture) — the engineering tradition
  that /is/ this substrate: an append-only event log as the source of
  truth, all state as disposable projections rebuilt from the log. It
  brings mature, hard-won failure-mode knowledge (snapshot drift,
  replay cost) and makes the whole architecture legible to an engineer
  in one sentence.
- *Architecture Decision Records* (Nygard, 2011) — the engineering
  orthopraxy of the decision ledger: lightweight records binding each
  decision to its context, alternatives, and consequences, superseded
  rather than edited. A closer ancestor of the roadmap's ledger
  stratum than event sourcing itself.
- *Jay Kreps* ("The Log," 2013) and *Yohei Nakajima* ("The Log is the
  Agent," 2026) — the log generalised, then applied to the machines
  themselves: an ordered, append-only log as the backbone from which
  every other representation is derived; and the agent-engineering
  world independently converging on that substrate for language-model
  agents — log as source of truth, state as disposable projection,
  end-to-end lineage — with no equivalent of Principle II +anywhere in
  sight+. /(Narrowed 2026-10-03: human nodes are now in sight in that
  world, but none is this one —/
  /the verifier of Gilda & Gilda (2026) is external to the generation
  loop and explicitly need not stay human;/
  /the industrial human-in-the-loop of 2026 allocates the human by the
  stakes of the action;/
  /and the non-delegability argued in the philosophy of technology
  (Torrecilla-Pinero, 2026)/ /is ontological and scoped to
  dignity-touching domains./ /None allocates by authorship with a
  fence that moves.)/ The clearest external proof that the two
  principles are separable, and that the second is this practice's
  actual claim.
- *James C. Scott* (/Seeing Like a State/, 1998) — the cautionary
  corpus: imposed top-down legibility schemes that flatten reality and
  break against it. What happens at civilizational scale when the map
  is allowed to outrank the territory.
- *Carlo Ginzburg* ("Clues," 1979) — the evidential paradigm:
  reconstructing reality from traces, clues, and symptoms, as the
  hunter, the physician, and the historian do. The trans-disciplinary
  pedigree of "traces are the source of truth."
- *George Box* ("Science and Statistics," 1976) — "all models are
  wrong, some are useful": the pragmatist license for the disposable
  map, in one page.
- *C. S. Peirce* — abduction: inference to the best explanation from
  the traces. The logical form of what the comparator does; structural,
  not decorative.

** The judgment node: why one act stays human

- *Aristotle* (/Nicomachean Ethics/) — /praxis/ versus /poiesis/:
  action whose end is in the doing versus production whose end is the
  product. The allocation policy, twenty-three centuries early:
  delegate the poiesis, keep the praxis.
- *Michael Polanyi* (/Personal Knowledge/, 1958; /The Tacit Dimension/,
  1966) — "we know more than we can tell": why the judgment draws on
  situated knowledge that cannot be fully externalised into any trace.
- *Hubert Dreyfus* (/Mind Over Machine/) — expert judgment as
  intuitive, embodied, and non-representational; the novice-to-expert
  ladder as an account of what the comparator's skill /is/ and how it
  forms.
- *Donald Schön* (/The Reflective Practitioner/, 1983) —
  reflection-in-action: the working theory of the practice's reflective
  beat and the inspect-before-signing discipline.
- *John Dewey* (/Logic: The Theory of Inquiry/) — knowing-as-doing;
  inquiry as the active reconstruction of a problematic situation; the
  demolition of the spectator theory of knowledge. The practice's
  native philosophical tradition.
- *James J. Gibson* (/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception/) —
  direct perception and affordances: the empirical case that agents
  pick up reality rather than consulting inner maps of it.
- *Christopher Alexander* (/The Timeless Way of Building/) — organic,
  piecemeal, reality-corrected growth against the imposed master plan:
  the live emergent document, in the register of making.
- *Nassim Taleb* (/Skin in the Game/; /Antifragile/) — the binding of
  decision to consequence as the formal core of signing; and a third
  systems behaviour beyond balancing and reinforcing — gaining from
  stressors — as the adversarial temperament the practice needs.
- *The Stoic discipline of assent* (Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus) —
  assent only to the evident: the oldest statement of the sign-off as a
  practiced discipline rather than a held belief. Kept from a longer
  contemplative shortlist because it alone names a move the practice
  actually makes; the rest of that tradition enriches the felt sense of
  the work but grounds no distinction, and is honestly classed as
  resonance, not lineage.
- *Jesús Torrecilla-Pinero* ("Judgment Cannot Be Delegated: A
  Subject-Preserving Framework for AI Governance," 2026) — the
  non-delegability claim stated as an ontological limit: moral
  judgment is constitutively personal, so delegating it does not
  transfer it but dissolves the subject who judges; and the
  observation that a human who rubber-stamps model output satisfies
  the tracing condition of Meaningful Human Control while exercising
  nothing that deserves the name of judgment — fragility one, from
  the governance side. Kept for the contrast it sharpens: he draws
  the line by ontology, for dignity-touching domains; this practice
  draws it by authorship, for answerable work, and holds it as a
  policy whose location moves. Same fence, two different reasons for
  it.

** The augmentation tradition: extending the witness

- *Vannevar Bush* ("As We May Think," 1945) — the Memex: external
  associative memory as an extension of recall.
- *J. C. R. Licklider* ("Man-Computer Symbiosis," 1960) and *Douglas
  Engelbart* ("Augmenting Human Intellect," 1962) — the canonical
  statements of extend-don't-substitute; this manifesto is their thesis
  re-derived for the language-model era.
- *Edwin Hutchins* (/Cognition in the Wild/, 1995) and *Clark &
  Chalmers* ("The Extended Mind," 1998) — cognition lives in artifacts:
  the checkpoint document is not a record of thinking but a part of it.
- *Endel Tulving* (encoding specificity, 1973) and *Godden & Baddeley*
  (context-dependent recall, 1975) — the memory science that the
  retrieval design is /modeled on/: recall works best when retrieval
  cues match encoding cues. An inspiration honestly labeled as analogy,
  not implementation.

** The loyal opposition: kept because they push back

- *Judea Pearl* (/The Book of Why/, 2018) — the sharpest scope-limit:
  the loop self-corrects against /observable/ reality, but causal
  structure is not always observable. Where drift is causal-structural,
  re-contacting traces will not reveal it.
- *Hayden White* (/Metahistory/, 1973) — the prosecutor of the fidelity
  claim: every narrative emplots its facts; the "faithful, neutral
  account" is always already a structured argument. Read against the
  chronicle, not for it.
- *Kahneman & Klein* ("Conditions for Intuitive Expertise," 2009) — the
  constraint on the human node: tacit judgment is trustworthy only in
  environments with valid cues and fast feedback. Both a warning and,
  read closely, the strongest argument /for/ the loop: it is what
  creates the conditions under which the comparator's intuition can be
  trusted at all.
- *Atul Gawande* (/The Checklist Manifesto/, 2009) — the evidence that
  in well-modeled regimes, standing prescriptive maps beat situated
  judgment. The counter-tradition that forces axis two into the
  practice instead of leaving it as an unexamined blind spot.
- *Lisanne Bainbridge* ("Ironies of Automation," 1983) — the classic
  statement that automation makes the human's remaining role harder,
  not easier: the operator removed from routine work loses exactly the
  situational awareness the exceptional cases demand, and the better
  the automation, the less informative the experience of supervising
  it. The structural cause behind fragility five, four decades before
  language models.
- *Ethan Mollick* (/Co-Intelligence/, 2024) — the rival allocation
  policy: delegate by measured capability, mapping the "jagged
  frontier" empirically and handing off whatever falls inside it. Kept
  because it names the live alternative this practice refuses:
  capability-allocation moves the fence wherever the machine improves,
  with no principled stopping point; authorship-allocation predicts
  exactly that drift and names what must not cross regardless of
  capability.
- *Subbarao Kambhampati* ("Can Large Language Models Reason and
  Plan?", 2024; "LLMs Can't Plan, But Can Help Planning in LLM-Modulo
  Frameworks", with others, 2024) — the generate-test-critique loop
  with the human deliberately kept out of the inner iteration: placed
  at specification and model acquisition, with a sound external
  verifier certifying the result. Supplies the Clever Hans reading of
  the correction rate and the test for whether an act has crossed:
  who certified the answer, and what the prompts carried. Opposition
  because it draws the human out of the inner loop where fragility
  five draws them back in to stay calibrated; the two are reconciled
  by nesting, his loop inside the delegable layer, this one around it.
- *Sankalp Gilda and Shlok Gilda* ("AI-Assisted Engineering Should
  Track the Epistemic Status and Temporal Validity of Architectural
  Decisions," 2026) — the oracle rule formalised from the engineering
  side: every machine-proposed decision carries an epistemic level
  (conjecture, substantiated, corroborated) and an evidence expiry,
  and their "Transformer Mandate" requires that whoever finalises a
  decision be external to the generation loop. Opposition because the
  mandate is explicitly about separation, not about the human:
  "currently, this necessitates a human; in future multi-agent
  systems, the role could theoretically be filled by an independent
  verifier agent." What moves in both is the fence; what differs is
  the anchor. Their human stands at ratification until a machine can
  verify soundly, and then steps aside: allocation by capability, in
  Kambhampati's shape. This practice moves the line between poiesis
  and praxis as machines improve, but pins the choice and the
  signature to answerability and to the value of the doing, which no
  capability unlocks. Their evidence decay is a clock standing in for
  re-contact: a decision expires after a set time and is re-validated.
  Here a decision never expires by the calendar; it is overturned the
  moment a changed world-trace reaches it, because it is a derived
  representation like any map and the traces win. The clock is the
  right device where re-contact is too costly to run on every pass;
  where it is cheap, re-derivation subsumes it.
- *Human-in-the-loop by stakes* (the 2026 agent-platform pattern:
  "agent proposes, a person disposes," approval gated on the risk or
  irreversibility of the action, every intervention logged) — the
  third allocation policy, beside authorship and capability: the
  human is placed where the consequences are large, not where the
  act is theirs. Kept because it is the one that ships, and because
  it is the arrangement Green (2022) found produces the ritual: a
  checkpoint chosen by stakes invites a signature chosen by habit.

What is genuinely new here is none of the elements — it is their
convergence onto one substrate at one moment: legible digital traces
that are simultaneously the practitioner's durable thinking, the
machine's working context, and the work's authoritative state, made
practicable by machines finally able to read, distil, and narrate
traces at negligible cost.

* Genus and name

** A practice, not a methodology

The right genus for this thing is *practice*, and the precise term is
*orthopraxy*. The orthodoxy/orthopraxy distinction is about where
authority lives: orthodoxy locates correctness in right belief — a
doctrine you assent to; orthopraxy locates it in right practice —
conduct done rightly, with doctrine downstream or absent.

Run the practice through that lens. There is no authoritative doctrine
to conform to — the map is explicitly disposable, refused as a source
of truth; that is the anti-creed move. Authority lives in the doing and
its residue: in traces (what was actually done) and in the repeated
discipline (re-contact, re-derive, judge, sign). What makes an account
genuine rather than counterfeit is not assent to a proposition but
whether the witnessing act was actually performed. And the isomorphism
is exact:

#+begin_quote
orthodoxy : orthopraxy :: plan-first : reality-first
#+end_quote

Orthodoxy lets doctrine generate conduct; plan-first work lets the plan
generate action. The practice refuses both inversions in the same
gesture.

One refinement keeps the term honest: this is not a behaviorist
orthopraxy of outward conduct. The act it protects is an /interior,
situated judgment/ — so the precise name for the genus is an
*orthopraxy of judgment*: authority lives in practice rather than in
any doctrine, but the practice that carries the authority is an act of
discernment with an irreducible inside.

** The name

*The reality-first practice.* The name foregrounds the timeless half —
the direction of authority between reality and its representations —
and lets the era-dependent half (where exactly the automation fence
sits this decade) remain what it is: a policy, revised as machines
change, inside a frame that does not.

Digital traces are the practice's substrate; automation is its
accelerant; neither is its principle. The principle is the explorer's:
the chart is always redrawn from the coastline.

* The extraction

One passage to carry into any new project:

#+begin_quote
Build the work around plain-text, append-only traces that are
simultaneously your durable thinking, the machine's working context,
and the project's authoritative state. Treat every representation built
on them as disposable: re-derived from the traces on every contact, and
overruled by the traces wherever they disagree. Hand the machine every
act whose value is independent of who performed it — surfacing,
orienting, distilling, narrating, scaffolding, and the bulk of the
visible labour — because that friction is what otherwise kills the
practice. Keep exactly one node: the situated judgment that turns
surfaced reality into a committed choice, registered before the machine
speaks and signed after adversarial inspection. Hold the fence as a
norm and a policy, not a theorem: its location moves as machines
improve; the direction of authority — reality over representation —
does not. Set the artifact's prescriptiveness by the shape of the work:
a live, fused, re-derived roadmap for bounded delivery; a pure
append-only spine for open-ended accumulation; an earned checklist
where the domain is charted to death. And measure the practice by what
it claims: faster resumption, fewer phantoms, an account that can be
defended years later by the one who signed it.
#+end_quote

* Provenance of this manifesto

This manifesto distils a longer comparative essay that analyzed the
convergent design of the originating projects, together with a critical
review of that essay. The review's corrections are applied here rather
than appended: the two candidate first principles are presented as a
conjunction instead of ranked (the essay's two halves had crowned each
in turn); the requisite-variety argument is stated as a criterion
rather than a theorem; the memory-science framing is labeled as the
analogy it is; the convergence evidence is claimed at honest strength
and its single-practitioner limit stated; the anchoring fragility and
the plan-primacy counter-tradition are added; and the lineage was
re-tiered under an explicit inclusion test, with the additions that
test demanded (Aristotle, Popper, Boyd, Deming, event sourcing,
Kahneman & Klein, Gawande) and the prunings it forced.

The document was first signed on 2026-06-07. A revision of 2026-08-30 integrated two further sources. A working
conversation on oracle use and on the calibration of the judge
supplied the oracle rule, the fifth fragility, and the second
balancing loop pointed at the comparator. A survey of adjacent work
supplied the Bainbridge entry, the rival capability-allocation policy
(Mollick), the ADR and log-as-agent lineage, the first external
evidence now logged under Falsifiability (Lee et al. 2025, Kosmyna et
al. 2025, Green 2022), and the multi-signer scope limit. One sentence
— the babysitting-tax claim — was corrected by dated strikethrough
rather than rewritten, per the practice's own rule.

A revision of 2026-09-27 added the Clever Hans reading to fragility
five and to the correction-rate measure, and a lineage entry, from
Subbarao Kambhampati's 2024 essay "Can Large Language Models Reason
and Plan?" (Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences), which
carries the argument the LLM-Modulo paper of the same year states in
one clause: the manifesto had read the correction rate only at its low
end, and so named the machine's work passing as the judge's but never
the judge's work passing as the machine's. A second reading of the
source, before signing, moved the crossing test from the timing of

A revision of 2026-10-03 narrowed one lineage claim by dated

Per the practice itself, this document is a derived view, not a source
of truth. It is downstream of, and disposable relative to, the lived
discipline it describes. If the manifesto and the practice ever
disagree, the practice wins and the manifesto is re-derived — never the
reverse. A practice that could not survive its own manifesto being
deleted was never a practice; it was a doctrine wearing a document.
